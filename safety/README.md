# ARGUS public safety overview

> [Open the ARGUS website](https://kelvinchan324.github.io/ARGUS-collaboration/) · [English](https://kelvinchan324.github.io/ARGUS-collaboration/en/)

This overview summarises the controls used for supervised ARGUS demonstrations
without publishing participant photographs, private contacts, venue-specific
details or signed records. It supports planning only and does not replace a
site-specific risk assessment or approval by the venue and responsible staff.

## Operating boundary

- One seated participant uses the cockpit under direct staff supervision.
- Live robot motion stays inside a clearly marked exclusion zone.
- Public visitors remain outside the robot workspace.
- Unpowered robots remain stabilised behind barriers.
- Only pre-approved, low-speed tasks are used in public demonstrations.

## Required roles

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Demonstration supervisor | Approves startup, shutdown and restart; coordinates with the venue |
| Cockpit attendant | Briefs the participant, fits the headset and monitors comfort and input |
| Robot-zone marshal | Keeps the exclusion zone and emergency access clear |

Every staff member has stop-work authority for unexpected motion, unstable
communications, zone intrusion, equipment damage or participant discomfort.

## Before motion

- Confirm the correct robot, hardware revision and software version.
- Inspect frames, fasteners, batteries, cables, sockets and enclosures.
- Verify the physical power cut and software emergency stop.
- Confirm speed, workspace, command and timeout limits.
- Test sensing, feedback and the direction of each axis at minimal power.
- Confirm that only one command source has control.
- Run a staff-only trial before admitting participants.

## Visitor-session sequence

1. Brief the participant on controls, boundaries and the stop signal.
2. Adjust the seat, headset and controls before enabling motion.
3. Confirm that the robot zone and emergency routes are clear.
4. Run one approved low-speed task with an attendant and marshal present.
5. Stop, neutralise, isolate when needed and reset before the next session.

## Immediate shutdown

1. Call “STOP” and activate the nearest emergency stop.
2. Keep visitors out and ask the participant to remain seated.
3. Isolate electrical power when safe.
4. Provide first aid or contact venue emergency services when required.
5. Secure damaged equipment and prevent reuse.
6. Record the event; restart only after the cause is resolved and checks repeat.

## Reassessment triggers

Repeat the risk assessment whenever the layout, robot, software, task, speed,
payload, power supply, control link, public access or weather conditions change.

## Repository privacy rule

Do not commit participant photographs, names, phone numbers, signatures,
private network addresses, venue access procedures or incident records to this
public repository. Store controlled operational records in an approved private
location.
